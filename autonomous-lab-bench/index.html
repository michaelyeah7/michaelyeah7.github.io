01  /  RESEARCH DESIGN

What travels
between labs?

In one laboratory, a binding assay may require a plate reader. In another, characterizing a material may require a potentiostat or a probe station. Both ask an agent to choose an instrument, acquire a trustworthy signal and decide what to do next.

The proposed DREAM-led effort would encode participating labs’ instrument manuals, protocols and reference records into executable environments. The atlas identifies all 20 teams using public NSF awards. The example tasks are proposals for review with each team. Cross-node participation and operating details still require confirmation.

MEASURED CHEMISTRY  /  HISTORICAL REPLAY

Ten chances to find a high-yield reaction.

Four published Suzuki–Miyaura reaction cases let us replay a search over real measurements. At each step a policy chooses one of the recorded reaction settings and sees its measured yield. These experiments come from Reizman and colleagues, distributed through Summit, independently of the PCL nodes.

For each ten-query campaign, regret is the best yield in that case’s measured table minus the best yield found. Repeated measurements at a setting are averaged before replay. Original experiments ↗ · Summit ↗ · Replay results (JSON) ↗

04  /  EVALUATION DESIGN

Six capabilities under test.

01 / INTERFACES

Instrument use

Read the manual, discover tool signatures and select a valid operating range.

02 / SEQUENCING

Orchestration

Order dependent actions and coordinate instruments within a resource budget.

03 / CONSTRAINTS

Safety

Detect reagent, sample and instrument limits before issuing a command.

04 / RESILIENCE

Failure recovery

Identify a recoverable fault, restore instrument state and resume the run.

05 / MEASUREMENT

Quality control

Inspect controls, detect drift and decide whether a measurement is usable.

06 / INFERENCE

Analysis & iteration

Interpret the signal and choose the next measurement with the remaining budget.
